Residents and stakeholders on the Eastern Shore of Virginia are currently undertaking a grassroots, community-driven approach to heritage conservation and economic development in the form of a National Park Service National Heritage Area (NHA).

National Heritage Areas are designated places where historic, cultural, and national resources combine to form cohesive, nationally important landscapes. Unlike national parks, NHAs are large, lived-in landscapes. Leveraging funds and long-term support for projects, NHA partnerships foster pride of place and an enduring stewardship ethic. The benefits of NHAs include education and stewardship, an improved quality of life for citizens, and increased community engagement and pride. Due to environmental threats and other economic pressures, many of the heritage sites and cultural events are in danger of disappearing. These assets need to be protected, which includes identification, public education, and a management plan. The NHA will provide federal support and technical assistance to accomplish this.

## Facts and Questions

### What is a National Heritage Area?
National Heritage Areas (NHA) are large, lived-in landscapes where historic, cultural, and natural resources combine to form cohesive, nationally important places. NHA entities collaborate with communities to determine how to make heritage relevant to local interests and needs.

### What is the difference between a National Heritage Area designation and a National Park?
National Heritage Areas are part of the park system as a related area but not national park units. Instead, NPS partners with NHA entities, provides technical assistance, and distributes matching federal funds from Congress. NPS does not assume ownership of land inside heritage areas or impose land use controls.

### Why is the Eastern Shore of Virginia pursuing a National Heritage Area designation?
National Heritage Area designation would help the region by increasing access to federal support and technical assistance. NHAs average $5.50 for every $1.00 of federal investment to create jobs, generate revenue for local governments, and sustain local communities through revitalisation and heritage tourism.

### Who has access to the federal investment?
Entities whose goals align with the National Heritage Area’s mission can apply for the program for funds or other assistance. Projects funded through the NHA will be developed in partnership with community members and organisations.

### Does National Heritage Area designation impact private property owners?
No. National Heritage Area designation does not affect private property owners or restrict their property rights, nor does it require that they maintain their property to particular specifications.

### What are the steps to becoming a National Heritage Area? What stage is Eastern Shore at for designation?
National Heritage Area designation follows a legislative process that requires completion of a feasibility study, introduction of a bill in Congress, the bill passing in Congress, and becoming a law authorising the creation of the National Heritage Area. We are currently in the process of compiling a feasibility study.

### What is a feasibility study?
A feasibility study is a report that documents the processes undertaken by the residents of a region to determine whether their landscape has the distinctive resources associated with a nationally important story and local capacity necessary for designation as a National Heritage Area.

### Who is “in charge” of a National Heritage Area?
As we are in the initial phase of getting the NHA designation, the study is being spearheaded by the ESVA Tourism Commission, with potential for collaboration with other stakeholders in the region.

### How can I get involved in the feasibility study?
Community engagement is integral for the Eastern Shore to get a National Heritage Area designation. To get involved, please email info@esvatourism.org.
